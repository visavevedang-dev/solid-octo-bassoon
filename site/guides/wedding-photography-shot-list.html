Shooting the day

How to Build a Wedding Photography Shot List

A shot list isn't there to make you a better photographer on the day — it's there so you don't forget the one photo a family will ask about for the rest of their lives. The best shot lists are short, ordered by when they'll actually happen, and built to survive a schedule that's already running fifteen minutes late.

Structure it by moment, not by subject

A list sorted alphabetically or by "family," "couple," "details" is hard to use mid-event. Sort it by when it happens instead:

  • Getting ready — details (rings, invitation, dress, shoes), candid prep shots, first look if planned.
  • Ceremony — processional, vows, ring exchange, first kiss, recessional.
  • Family & group formals — grouped by combination, in the order people are likely to be available.
  • Couple portraits — golden hour or a dedicated window if the schedule allows it.
  • Reception — entrance, speeches, first dance, cake cutting, send-off.

The family formals list is where things go wrong

Group photos take longer than anyone budgets for, mostly because people wander off between combinations. Two things fix most of this:

  1. Name the exact combinations in advance — "immediate family," "bride's side extended," "both families" — agreed with the couple beforehand, not improvised on the day.
  2. Assign a point of contact — a family member or planner whose job is to physically round people up for each combination while you shoot.

Leave room for what isn't on the list

The best wedding photos are often the ones nobody planned for. A shot list should cover what absolutely cannot be missed — it shouldn't try to script the whole day. Treat it as a safety net for the non-negotiable moments, and shoot everything else as it happens.

Notes belong with the booking, not in your head

Shot lists, family-formal combinations, and any notes from a pre-wedding call are only useful if you can find them fast on the day — not buried in a notes app under a different couple's name. Keeping them attached to the specific booking means you're never trying to remember which chat thread had the details.
